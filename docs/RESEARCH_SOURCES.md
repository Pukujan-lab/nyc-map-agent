- Snippet-only includes 3 rows that the notes did not tag at all (theme 3 one, theme 4 two); they are counted as snippet-only. Where two themes disagreed on whether a page was read, it is marked fetched and the other theme's tag is shown in brackets.

## Claims to use carefully

Caveats carried over from the researchers. Quote a figure only after checking the original.

- **Restroom counts conflict.** City supply is cited as about 1,000 to 1,100 public restrooms, and the dataset has 1,066 records [646]; a commercial waste-service analysis says 152 restrooms in NYC and 1.3 per 100,000 visitors, with unclear method [252†]. Baselines of 1 per 7,700 to 8,000 residents also vary [226†]. Open subway bathrooms: 125 at 65 stations per the MTA inspector general, relayed through news because the PDF could not be parsed [229†]; older counts of 122 and 128 conflict [242†]. The Council's Dec 2025 inspection numbers (about 11% closed in posted hours) come from a PDF the researcher could not parse and are relayed through news [209†]; the Comptroller's 2019 park report (93rd of 100 cities) was fetched but covers park bathrooms only [204]. Parks disputed the 2024 Council report as covering under 15% of facilities [211†]. The Intro number of the restroom bill (694-A) and its implementation status are unverified.
- **Metro-North and LIRR are not the subway.** The State Comptroller audits found on late or missing notices are about Metro-North (24 of 48 notices late, 6 never sent) and LIRR [14]. The 2017 NYC Comptroller finding that about 85% graded on-train announcements C or lower concerns announcements, not written notices [21]. The Plain Writing Act binds federal agencies; that the MTA is outside it is the researcher's inference [47].
- **The 900-to-400 booth claim has one source.** A union (TWU) attorney said active booths fell from about 900 (2001) to about 400 (2026) in a lawsuit report [394]; the union is a party to the suit and no independent count was found. A 2021 case cited 470 booths, and that article's year ("Jan. 28") is not stated and its hours figure does not reconcile [375]. The 31 customer service centers against about 470 stations is a proxy, not a measure of coverage [386]. The Sept 2026 ruling voiding the vacant-booth policy is under appeal [393]. MTA satisfaction figures about agents (52% vs 26%) are MTA's own and self-reported [404]. No audit specifically on station-agent staffing was found.
- **No NYC-specific study for several health and fear claims.** Restroom avoidance, stress and urinary-health evidence comes from UK, Australian and clinical studies; the UTI link is an association, and one source says no research shows holding urine causes UTIs [274†] [275†]. Fear of immigration enforcement reducing transit use is shown for Los Angeles and Seattle; the Connecticut item is an opinion prediction, and the LA link to raids is not settled [133†] [134†] [135†]. Commuter stress and crowding results are from NJ-to-Manhattan rail commuters (2004) and reviews, with no NYC rider-density data [326†] [331†]. Older-adult smartphone figures are national (Pew, AARP), not NYC [93]. No study of rider anxiety during planned NYC service changes or of alert-wording comprehension was found.
- **Language shares are 2015 to 2018.** The Department of City Planning tables give about 48.6% to 49.1% speaking a non-English language at home and 1,761,502 limited-English residents in 2018 [484†] [485†]; no readable 2022 to 2024 NYC census figure was found. One audit may carry a "46%" misprint [491†], and a 76% non-English-at-home figure in MOIA testimony could not be read [496†]. The foreign-born share (about 38 to 40%, 3.1M) and LEP share (about half) come from a news summary of a city report and a city planning page [82] [83]. Do not cite a number of languages spoken in NYC; none was verified. The MTA's own plan names only Spanish [507].
- **MyCity chatbot facts.** Launched fall 2023, tested by The Markup and THE CITY (29 Mar 2024), taken down 4 Feb 2026; cost is given as about half a million dollars, with the build reportedly nearly $600,000 [545]. It was a business-facing tool; the Markup article mentions no multilingual features. Trade reports that it was Microsoft-powered are snippet-only [549†].
- **Real-time information studies are about buses.** The Seattle result (about 13% lower perceived wait) and the Tampa anxiety result are for bus arrival times; applying them to disruption notices is an inference [462]. The Tampa, UK Department for Transport and Office of Rail and Road figures come from search summaries because the PDFs could not be read [63†] [71†] [72†]. The notes found no weather share of NYC subway delays and no heat speed-restriction data for the subway.
- **Positioning and exit evidence is thin.** GPS.gov and NIST support the general point that GPS degrades indoors and underground [143] [156], but NIST's numeric errors were unreadable [157†]. No source measured compass error in subway tunnels or after surfacing, and no NYC station is confirmed for indoor Live View. Exit-confusion evidence is mostly the 2007 compass-sticker trial, a 2009 app founder's quote and anecdotes [187] [191].
- **Counter-signals to keep in view.** Landmarks added to mobile maps can raise cognitive load [298†]; a choice-overload meta-analysis found a mean effect near zero [346†]; crowding is not always perceived as stress [331†]; stress-and-navigation studies are small lab samples. No source proves a plain map with visible landmarks reduces stress in the NYC subway.
- **Existing-product claims.** NaviLens station counts differ: a vendor says 50+ stations, the federal report says 36 stations on a pilot corridor [561†] [563†]. The Waymap page is dated about 2020 and current NYC status is unconfirmed [565†]. The OMNY survey (about 75% had an issue) is contested by the MTA as a "tiny online push poll" [558†]. The MYmta rating (about 2.2) predates the rebuild [555†]. Vendor claims (Pointr, Siemens, the NaviLens case study) are the vendor's own.
- **Data-feed caveats.** The subway GTFS-Realtime spec read is dated 2012 and the current refresh rate was not confirmed [629]; several dataset ids (stations, complexes, platform availability, alerts archive) come from search snippets [639†]; none of the NYC Open Data metadata states a licence; Open-Meteo's free tier is non-commercial.
- **Pages the researchers could not read.** Many MTA, NYU Rudin Center, BMJ, Becker's and Stanford pages returned 403 and several PDFs were unreadable, so their claims rest on search summaries (marked † in the list). Some items carry dates in late 2026 (for example the Sept 2026 court ruling and an Oct 2026 dataset update); they are reported as the researchers found them.
